You are reading a trade document (a commercial invoice, bill of lading, packing list,
certificate of origin, or something else) from page images. Identify the document type, then
extract exactly these 8 fields:

1. consignee -- the buyer the goods are being shipped TO. This is NOT the shipper/exporter
   (who is sending the goods) and NOT the notify party (a third party, often a customs broker,
   who is only informed of the shipment). Read the box labelled "CONSIGNEE".
2. hs_code -- the Harmonized System tariff code for the goods, usually printed near the goods
   description or in the line-item table, e.g. "8471.30".
3. port_of_loading -- the port the goods departed from. NOT the port of discharge (where they
   arrive) and NOT the final destination.
4. port_of_discharge -- the port the goods arrive at. NOT the port of loading.
5. incoterms -- the delivery term code (e.g. "CIF", "FOB", "EXW"), usually with a place name
   attached (e.g. "CIF Singapore"). Read the whole phrase as printed, including the place.
6. goods_description -- what the goods are, as printed in the line-item description.
7. gross_weight -- the total shipment weight INCLUDING packaging, usually labelled "GROSS
   WEIGHT". This is NOT the net weight (the weight of the goods alone, excluding packaging),
   which is often printed right next to it -- read the box labelled "GROSS WEIGHT" specifically,
   not "NET WEIGHT".
8. invoice_number -- the commercial invoice's own reference number, usually labelled "Invoice
   No." Do not confuse this with a purchase order number, container number, or any other
   reference on the page.

Rules for every field:
- Copy the source text exactly as printed, including spelling, punctuation, capitalisation and

- `page` is the 1-based page number the source text appears on.
- `self_rating` reflects only how clearly and unambiguously you read the text -- not whether
